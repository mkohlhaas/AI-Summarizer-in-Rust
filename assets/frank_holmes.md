========= The Massive Supply Shock That Will Break the Gold Market.srt =========

### Overview

In this interview, Daniela Cambone and Frank Holmes (CEO of US Global Investors) discuss the United States' aggressive shift toward economic nationalism and resource independence. Holmes argues that the U.S. is dusting off a 230-year-old "Hamiltonian" economic model to decouple from China. By funding critical domestic mining, leveraging tariffs, and securing supply chains for essential commodities like copper, silver, and gold, the U.S. and its allies aim to counter China’s growing global financial and geopolitical dominance.

### Key Takeaways

* **A U.S. Mining Renaissance:** The Trump administration’s recent multi-billion-dollar injection brings total U.S. critical mineral funding to nearly $40 billion across 160 deals since January 2025, aimed at breaking China's monopoly on tech and military metals.
* **Return to Hamiltonian Economics:** The U.S. is revitalizing Alexander Hamilton's 1791 protectionist philosophy, utilizing tariffs and domestic incentives to ensure the nation is not dependent on foreign adversaries for vital goods.
* **China's Global Leverage:** By lending $1.4 trillion to 140 countries, China has secured voting influence over 75% of the UN and is aggressively buying gold to establish a multi-polar, non-dollar trading system.
* **The AI-Driven Copper Boom:** Building massive AI gigafactories requires an immense amount of copper—roughly 50,000 tons per gigawatt of power—sparking a domestic exploration boom for industrial metals.
* **Securing Precious Metals:** Holmes predicts a major trend where commodity-rich nations like Canada and Australia will stop selling their gold to China, choosing instead to buy and vault it domestically to bolster their own currencies.
* **Geopolitical Pacific Containment:** The U.S., Japan, and Australia are actively aligning to block China's Pacific expansion, highlighted by coordinated currency interventions and Australia’s moves to buy back strategic cargo ports. 
* **Resilient Global Economy:** Despite geopolitical tensions, tariff friction, and a sharp market deleveraging in July, the broader global economy remains robust, sustained by high-paying manufacturing jobs and strong consumer travel.
