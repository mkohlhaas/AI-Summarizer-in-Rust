### Overview

In this interview, sports scientist Prof. Dr. Jürgen Gieing explains how
targeted strength training acts as a powerful "fountain of youth" to reverse
biological aging. He highlights how muscles function as a major metabolic and
endocrine organ, and outlines how brief, high-intensity training paired with
the right nutritional strategies can dramatically improve long-term health,
mobility, and cognitive function.

### Key Points

* **"Use It or Lose It":** Muscle loss is a primary driver of aging; regular
resistance training not only halts this decline but actively reverses
biological aging processes.
* **The Body's Carbon Sink:** Muscles are the body's largest storage site for
carbohydrates. Losing muscle mass directly leads to insulin resistance,
elevated blood sugar, and fat accumulation (such as fatty liver disease).
* **Brain-Muscle Connection:** Strength training first improves neuronal
pathways and coordination—which prevents falls—and has been shown to lower the
risk of dementia and Alzheimer's in seniors.
* **Efficiency Over Volume:** High-Intensity Training (HIT) taken to muscle
failure (one clean set per exercise, twice a week) is highly effective and more
time-efficient than traditional multi-set workouts.
* **Preserve Fast-Twitch Fibers:** Aging primarily degrades Type II
(fast-twitch) muscle fibers. Reaching muscular failure during workouts is
essential to keep these critical fibers and their controlling nerves alive.
* **Higher Protein Needs with Age:** Due to declining digestive efficiency,
older adults require nearly double the protein intake per meal compared to
younger individuals to trigger muscle synthesis.
* **Creatine as a Safe Booster:** A daily dose of 3–5g of creatine increases
physical energy, mitigates the cognitive effects of sleep deprivation, and
heavily benefits vegetarians who lack dietary creatine.
* **Natural Flexibility:** Training muscles through their full range of motion
while balancing opposing muscle groups (antagonists) naturally improves
flexibility, rendering dedicated stretching optional.
