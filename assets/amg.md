========= Tobias Moers AMG Wie aus einer kleinen Werkstatt eine Weltmarke.srt =========

### Overview

This transcript from the German automotive podcast *Alte Schule* features an in-depth interview with Tobias Moers, the influential engineer and former CEO of Mercedes-AMG. Moers details his career trajectory, beginning with his early work on an pioneering 1990s electric vehicle startup, through the chaotic early days of AMG as a small tuning garage, to its evolution into a highly structured, globally successful sports car manufacturer. 

### Core Points

* **Electric Beginnings:** Before entering the high-performance world, Moers worked in the early 1990s on the "Hotzenblitz," one of Germany's first purpose-built electric vehicle projects, sponsored by Ritter Sport.
* **Early AMG Improvisation:** Joining AMG in 1994, Moers faced a complete lack of manufacturing infrastructure; the team had to sneak into the Sindelfingen Mercedes plant at night to manually write down part numbers to create their very first bill of materials.
* **"One Man, One Engine" Origin:** AMG’s famous assembly philosophy was actually a point of contention; Moers originally pushed for a more efficient, timed assembly line, but was overridden by Friedrich Aufrecht, who insisted on a single technician taking ownership of each engine.
* **The Daimler Takeover:** The 1999 buyout by Daimler was a turning point. Led by Wolfgang Bernhard, AMG preserved its unique identity while securing the corporate backing needed to expand its vehicle portfolio.
* **Supercharged Growing Pains:** The leap to the 500-horsepower supercharged V8 engines presented massive engineering challenges, including melting engines in Nardò and a desperate, last-minute search for a fuel pump capable of meeting the engine's extreme demand.
* **Developing the SLS AMG:** Built from a "blank sheet of paper," early drivetrain prototypes for the iconic gullwing supercar actually used modified Dodge Vipers purchased from Chrysler as test mules.
* **Saving the G-Class:** Moers successfully fought G-Class traditionalists in Graz to install an independent front suspension on the modern G63, proving to skeptical test drivers that single-wheel suspension improved both on-road handling and off-road speed.
* **The 4-Cylinder Shift:** Addressing the controversial modern hybrid 4-cylinder AMG setups, Moers explains that stringent EU7 emissions laws left the engineering team with virtually no choice but to hybridize and downsize.
