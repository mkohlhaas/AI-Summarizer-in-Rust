### Overview
In this episode of *Capital Cosm*, host Danny and guest J. Adrian Day discuss shifts in global markets. They explore signs of a bottom in precious metals, the historically low valuations of mining and energy stocks, a structural rotation out of overvalued US tech into value and foreign equities, and emerging cracks in the debt and currency markets.

### Key Takeaways

* **Precious Metals Bottoming:** Increasing inflows into gold and silver mining ETFs (GDX, GDXJ) and strong moves in speculative "white metals" (platinum, palladium) indicate that retail money is returning and a market bottom is likely in.
* **Mining Stocks Historically Cheap:** Major gold producers are trading in the lowest quartile of their 50-year valuation history. Companies like Agnico Eagle are producing massive free cash flow with clean balance sheets and incredibly wide profit margins.
* **Oil & Gas Multi-Year Buy:** Despite short-term price drops, structural underinvestment and the unrealistic narrative of a rapid transition away from fossil fuels make oil majors (like Exxon and Chevron) compelling multi-year investments. 
* **Market Propped Up by Passive Flows:** The S&P 500 continues to defy odds, driven by automated 401(k) passive inflows and a halt in Federal Reserve rate hikes, though smart money is rotating out of overvalued tech into small caps.
* **Rattled Debt Markets:** Spiking 10-year and 30-year yields reveal that the bond market is pricing in a weaker dollar and a lack of long-term buyers, even as the stock market celebrates a paused Fed.
* **Failing Currency Interventions:** Recent coordinated efforts by the Bank of Japan and the US Treasury to prop up the Japanese Yen (JPY) have yielded diminishing returns, with sharp spikes quickly crashing back down.
* **International Value Arbitrage:** Non-US equities—particularly commodity-exporting emerging markets (excluding South Korea) and smaller hubs like Singapore and Brazil—present historic relative value and are poised to outperform the US.
